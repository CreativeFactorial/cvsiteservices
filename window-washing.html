Why glass gets dirty faster here

Three things work against windows in the Gallatin Valley, and they are not the same three that a window cleaner in Seattle deals with.

Hard water. Valley water carries a lot of dissolved mineral. Every time a sprinkler head throws spray onto a south- or west-facing window and the sun bakes it off, it leaves the mineral behind. Do that for three summers and you get a cloudy haze that soap will not remove — it has to be treated, and past a certain point the glass is permanently etched.

Wind and grit. The valley moves a lot of dust, and in spring it moves whatever was spread on the roads all winter. That fine grit ends up in the bottom track of every window, where it holds moisture against the frame.

Smoke. Late-summer smoke settles a fine oily film on glass that is noticeably harder to cut than dust. A wash right after the air clears makes more difference than one in mid-August.

About hard-water spots

Mineral deposits from sprinkler overspray are not dirt and a normal wash will not remove them. They need a separate abrasive or acidic treatment, which we quote on its own after looking at the glass.

We will tell you honestly if the glass is etched past the point treatment helps. At that stage the only fix is replacing the pane, and no amount of scrubbing changes it — we would rather say so than take your money for a service that will not work.

  • FrequencyTwice a year keeps each visit shorter and cheaper than one annual catch-up.

Common questions

How often should windows be washed?

Twice a year suits most homes here — once in spring after runoff and road sanding, once in fall before the weather turns. Houses with sprinklers hitting the glass, or a gravel road nearby, do better on a three-time schedule. Storefronts are usually monthly.

Do you do interior windows too?

Yes. Someone needs to be home or arrange access, and we use drop cloths and shoe covers inside. Plenty of customers do exterior-only in spring and both in fall.

Can you reach third-story windows?

Usually, with a water-fed pole. Some layouts need a ladder or a lift, and if a lift is required we will say so in the quote rather than surprise you with it.

What about the screens?

They come out, get washed, and go back in as part of a standard wash. If a screen is torn we will point it out, but we do not repair them.
